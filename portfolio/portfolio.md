## Portfolio statement

Kobald is the project I used to investigate a question that matters to me: how can an AI system be helpful without becoming unaccountable? I started with the ambitious idea of a local JARVIS-style assistant. Building it made me realise that intelligence is not the same as trust. My focus changed from “make the most capable AI I can” to “make and test a system where a human stays responsible for consequential action.”

The portfolio records that change in direction, not just the finished software. It includes decisions that worked, technical failures that made me rethink my confidence, feedback from people outside my project, and limitations I still need to address.

## My current judgement

I have made meaningful progress because I can point to a chain of decisions: I chose smaller, reviewable development phases; I made safety constraints part of the architecture; I used live testing and audits to challenge passing tests; and I sought equipment, funding and communication feedback when the original plan did not fit my resources.

My biggest limitation is that the technical feedback loop is still mostly made by me, my tools and published documentation. Those are valuable perspectives, but they are not independent human scrutiny. Before the final portfolio, I need to add a current system capture and a response from someone with relevant technical experience. Leaving that gap visible is part of my evaluation, because hiding it would make the portfolio less trustworthy than the system I am trying to design.
